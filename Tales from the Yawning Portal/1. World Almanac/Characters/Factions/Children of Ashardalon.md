A large cult led by [[Gulthias]], who was hoping to earn the favor of [[Ashardalon]] before his heart was cut out. After [[Dydd the Wise]] killed [[Ashardalon]], [[Gulthias]] tried to bring him back to this plane of existence using dark rituals.

[[Ashardalon]]'s death had a major impact on both [[The Sunless Citadel (Location)]] and the Children of Ashardalon as the [[Elf]] faction saw no definitive proof of being able to restore [[Ashardalon]]. Those at [[The Nightfang Spire]] still stayed strong in faith, however, because [[Gulthias]] spent most of his time there. When the heart of [[Ashardalon]] started beating again, hundreds of years after the mighty [[Dragon]] died, [[Gulthias]] and some of the cult members all became [[Undead]] in some fashion after drinking a strange red concoction within close proximity to the heart. [[Gulthias]] decided to go to [[The Sunless Citadel (Location)]] to retrieve a book, [[Treasures of the Fire Lords]] and (hopefully) finally revive [[Ashardalon]].

[[Gulthias]]' return to [[The Sunless Citadel (Location)]] was a huge surprise to everyone, because he had been turned into a [[Vampire]] and this caused a lot of controversy. [[Failed Dragonpriest, Stanley]] decided to also transform himself using the [[True Polymorph]] spell, which he found in [[Treasures of the Fire Lords]]. [[Gulthias]] was disgusted by this transformation, for it had failed and turned him into a troll. After being told that [[Gulthias]] had no need for a monstrous pet, he was magically imprisoned and guarded by [[Jot]] until many years later. 

Upon hearing rumors of his return, a [[Atlas Dyddson]] arrived at [[The Sunless Citadel (Location)]] to prevent [[Gulthias]] from reviving [[Ashardalon]]. The cult died down swiftly after the mysterious disappearance of [[Gulthias]], for none of them outside of [[The Sunless Citadel (Location)]] lived to hear that he had become [[The Gulthias Tree]]. when the [[Atlas Dyddson]] drove a wooden stake through the heart of the cults leader, causing the Citadel to sink as the stake took root.
